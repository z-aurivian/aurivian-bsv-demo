Merck's ESHRE 2026 satellite reached 2,400+ IVF specialists with a batch-consistency attack on urinary FSH. Two published datasets already rebut it — and most KOLs haven't seen them yet.

The rFSH vs urinary gonadotropin debate did not start at ESHRE 2026 — but Merck's satellite symposium reframed it. Their message: recombinant FSH offers superior batch-to-batch consistency, reducing cycle-to-cycle variability. The claim is clinically plausible but not supported by comparative live birth data. 01Clinical Data · IVFCARE India RCT

IVFCARE India: equivalent live birth rates in normal responders. The batch-consistency argument does not translate to clinical outcomes.

↑ Prioritised · Recurrence 4BSV-Sponsored RCT

The IVFCARE India RCT (NCT05412847, published Fertility & Sterility January 2026) enrolled 412 normal responders across 6 Indian IVF centres. Primary endpoint: live birth rate per cycle. Results: urinary HMG arm 38.2% vs recombinant FSH arm 37.9% (p=0.91, non-inferiority met). Secondary endpoints — number of oocytes retrieved, fertilisation rate, embryo quality — were statistically equivalent. The study design directly addresses the consistency objection: all urinary HMG batches were tested against the European Pharmacopoeia standard, with potency variation <2% across the cohort. Merck's batch-consistency framing applies to historical manufacturing standards — not to current BSV HMG supply.

"The equivalence data in normal responders is robust. For my typical IVF patient, the choice between urinary HMG and recombinant FSH should be driven by cost-effectiveness and patient access — not batch variability arguments that no longer apply to modern manufacturing."

02Meta-Analysis · Cochrane 2024

Cochrane 2024 update: no significant difference in live birth or OHSS rates between recombinant and urinary gonadotropins.

Cochrane Systematic Review▲ High Authority Evidence

The updated Cochrane meta-analysis (2024) synthesised 42 RCTs across 9,141 women comparing recombinant FSH versus urinary gonadotropins (HMG and uFSH) for IVF. Primary outcome: live birth or ongoing pregnancy per woman randomised. RR 1.01 (95% CI 0.97–1.06) — no statistically significant difference. OHSS rates: 3.8% vs 4.2% — again no significant difference. The Cochrane review is the highest-authority signal in the evidence base and directly contradicts the superiority framing in Merck's satellite. Intelligence Brief Luprodex · Leuprolide July 11, 2026 · 8:00 AM IST

ENDOCARE trial: the 3-month Luprodex protocol achieves equal pain control with half the injections. FOGSI 2026 data are shifting early-adopter KOLs — the broader network has not yet heard the story.

Endometriosis management with GnRH agonists has historically defaulted to monthly depot formulations. The ENDOCARE trial (completed, 12 Indian sites) directly compared Luprodex 3-month vs 6-month protocols for Stage II–IV endometriosis. Primary endpoint results were presented at FOGSI 2026: 3-month protocol achieved non-inferiority on pelvic pain scores (VAS at 24 weeks) while reducing injection burden from 6 to 2 over the treatment course. Early-adopter KOLs in the FOGSI network have begun recommending the 3-month protocol. The broader tier-2 prescriber network is not yet aware.

01Clinical Data · ENDOCARE Trial

ENDOCARE: VAS pelvic pain 4.1 (3-month) vs 4.0 (6-month) at 24 weeks. Non-inferiority met. Injection burden: 2 vs 6.

▲ FOGSI 2026 DataNon-Inferiority Confirmed

ENDOCARE enrolled 218 women with laparoscopically confirmed Stage II–IV endometriosis across 12 centres (AIIMS Delhi, KEM Mumbai, Bangalore Baptist, and 9 regional sites). Primary endpoint: VAS pelvic pain score at 24 weeks. Results: 3-month protocol arm 4.1 vs 6-month arm 4.0 (difference 0.1, 95% CI −0.4 to 0.6; non-inferiority margin 1.5 — met). Secondary endpoints: dysmenorrhoea VAS, deep dyspareunia VAS — all equivalent. Bone mineral density changes at 24 weeks: comparable between arms. The key differentiator is patient burden: 2 injections vs 6 over the treatment course, with 4 fewer hospital visits required in the 3-month arm. Patient satisfaction score (5-point Likert) was significantly higher in the 3-month arm (4.2 vs 3.8, p=0.03).

"The 3-month data are compelling for my patients who travel more than an hour to reach me. Same outcomes, dramatically better compliance. I've already switched my new-start endometriosis patients to the 3-month protocol."
